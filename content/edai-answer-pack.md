# SoldOut: EdAI Founder and Program Brief

## 1. Founder Bio

Dr. Azeez Idris is the Co-Founder and CEO of EdAI. He holds a PhD in Computer Science from Iowa State University, where his research focused on improving image classification with limited data. He previously worked as a Data Scientist II at Microsoft on SwiftKey. He is a Nigerian Muslim based in Redmond, Washington.

Azeez started EdAI to help young people become builders and founders using AI and Islamic values. EdAI evolved from teaching students about AI, to helping them build with AI, to guiding them toward products that address real problems and can be used by real people.

After being laid off from Microsoft, he chose to pursue EdAI full-time. He has described his ambition as building an accelerator for middle- and high-school students. His emphasis is on helping students identify useful problems, take responsibility for their work, and turn their abilities into value for others.

## 2. EdAI

### Mission

"Raising Muslim Teens as Builders and Founders."

EdAI combines AI, entrepreneurship, practical problem-solving, and Islamic values. Students work on understanding people's needs, building useful products, testing their ideas, and improving through evidence.

### What Makes EdAI Different

- Real problems and real users: Students investigate needs and seek feedback beyond the classroom.
- Building with AI: Students use technology to create apps, websites, tools, and other products.
- Faith guides decisions: Service, honesty, responsibility, and amanah inform what students build and how they build it.
- Evidence before assumptions: Customer conversations, testing, and feedback shape the next step.
- Founder development: The learning includes judgment, communication, outreach, and understanding customers.
- Practical instruction and mentorship: Azeez teaches the November online cohorts. Muslim technology professionals also mentor students; participation varies by cohort.

### What Students Build

Builder: Students explore a problem, identify who needs help, choose suitable tools, and build an early version of a useful product. They seek feedback and improve their work. An app, website, or tool may be an appropriate project, depending on the problem.

Founder: Students start with something they have already built. They investigate potential customers, test demand, try outreach and sales experiments, and improve their product using what they learn.

An idea alone belongs in Builder.

1. **What is EdAI?**
   EdAI helps young people become builders and founders using AI, practical problem-solving, and Islamic values. Students work on useful products, learn from real people, and improve through feedback.
2. **Who is this program for?**
   The November online cohorts are for Muslim students in grades 6–12. We choose between Builder and Founder based on the student's starting point.
3. **Does my child need coding experience?**
   No previous coding experience is required for Builder. Founder is for students who already have a first version of a product.
4. **What is the difference between Builder and Founder?**
   Builder helps a student move from an idea or problem toward a first functioning prototype. Founder helps a student with an existing product understand customers, test demand, and decide how to improve and grow it.
5. **What if my child does not have an idea yet?**
   Builder can suit students who are curious about technology or have noticed a problem they want to solve. Exploring the problem and understanding who needs help are part of the work.
